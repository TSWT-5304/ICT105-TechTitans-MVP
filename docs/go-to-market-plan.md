
# Digital Go-to-Market Plan

## Product / Prototype Name
PeerTutor – Student-to-Student Tutoring Marketplace

## Target Early Users
The initial MVP focuses on Rangsit University International College (RIC) students who need academic tutoring support, plus a small group of student tutors willing to be early listings on the platform. Without a few tutors registered first, students have nothing to search — so tutor recruitment should run just ahead of student outreach.

## Main Value Proposition
PeerTutor lets RSU students find, evaluate, and request a peer tutor in one trusted place, instead of searching scattered Facebook groups, LINE chats, and personal contacts.

## Channel Plan
| Channel | Target User | Message Angle | User Action Expected | Metric to Track | Why This Channel Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Class/course LINE or Facebook Messenger groups | Students currently searching for tutors | Stop the scattered search — one platform to find a tutor | Click demo link | Clicks / demo views | This is exactly where students said they already look for tutors (Lab 03 evidence) |
| Campus QR poster (library, study areas, RIC building) | Students preparing for exams or assignments | Find a tutor before your next exam | Scan QR to open landing page | QR scans / landing page views | Reaches students at the moment they feel the problem most |
| RIC student community Instagram/Facebook page | Students and prospective student tutors | Get tutoring help, or start tutoring, on your own campus | Visit landing page; tutors click "Apply as Tutor" | Post/story clicks | A channel both sides of the marketplace already follow |
| RSU student club or class announcement | RIC students (initial target segment) | Try the pilot tutoring platform built for RIC students | Sign up interest / try demo | Sign-ups, demo attempts | Matches the segment the MVP was scoped for, with a warmer, trusted introduction |

## Launch Sequence
1. Recruit 3–5 pilot student tutors and get their profiles live so search results aren't empty.
2. Prepare the landing page and confirm the demo/prototype link works end-to-end.
3. Share to the RIC class chat and student club channels first (highest trust, matches MVP scope).
4. Follow with the campus QR poster and Instagram/Facebook post once initial tutor listings exist.
5. Track responses using the acquisition metrics spreadsheet, form responses, and prototype/activity logs.
6. Collect feedback from early testers and revise the headline, CTA wording, or prototype flow if metrics are weak.

## Risk and Mitigation
- **Cold-start risk (no tutors, so nothing to search):** recruit and onboard a handful of tutors before promoting to students.
- **Low response from a single channel:** run at least three channels in parallel rather than depending on one.
- **Privacy concerns discouraging sign-ups:** reference the Data Handling Policy in the landing page's responsible data message so users know what is and isn't collected.
- **Confusion about payment being simulated:** clearly label the payment step as a demo/simulation to avoid unrealistic expectations, consistent with the "no unsupported feature promises" requirement.
